Why does FREQ exist?

Because mixing shouldn't have to be slow, expensive, and exhausting.

Kushal is a mixing and mastering engineer who has spent years working hands-on with music, studios, hardware, and software. His background spans piano, orchestral writing, jazz, production, mixing, and mastering; he built his own studio and spent years experimenting with the tools, workflows, and systems behind great mixes.

He had spent years seeing the same problem from both sides of the glass: mixing takes years to master, and hours to execute. The better the work gets, the more time it can take. So instead of accepting that as the price of making great music, he started looking for another way.

What started as a frustration became a five-year project.

An idea became an experiment. The experiment became a working system. And the system became FREQ.

It started with a simple question.

What if mixing could take less time without taking the human out of it?

The goal was never to replace the engineer. It was to remove the unnecessary friction around the work: to give an experienced mixer a faster way to make decisions, and to give an artist a better chance of getting a great-sounding record without spending weeks waiting for the next revision. To make the process feel closer to creating music, and further away from fighting the clock.

Then people started hearing it.

An early version of FREQ was already working by 2022. One of the first people to experience it was an orchestrator with a PhD in music. Instead of taking his usual fee, he wanted to keep a copy.

Another mixer sent over an unreleased track to test it. After hearing the result, his first question wasn't how it worked.

"When are you launching?"

Five years of building, breaking, refining, and listening.

There was no existing blueprint for exactly what was being built. So it came together piece by piece.

Algorithms Audio processing Workflow Testing More testing

And a lot of decisions about what to keep, what to remove, and what simply wasn't good enough. The product evolved through people actually using it: early testers became collaborators, feedback shaped the workflow, and FREQ slowly became what it is today.

So, what exactly is FREQ?

A different, one-of-a-kind way to approach mixing.

FREQ is a browser-based mixing and mastering platform built around a new way of thinking about space, balance, and sound. Instead of treating every track as another channel in a crowded session, FREQ gives the mix another dimension to work with. You still make the decisions. You still trust your ears. FREQ simply gives you a different set of tools to get there faster.

No AI deciding what your music should sound like.

Just algorithms built to respond to your music and your decisions.

01 · Depth

Music isn't only left and right.

FREQ introduces a front-to-back way of thinking about the mix, letting sounds occupy different depths and creating a more intentional sense of space.

02 · Control

FREQ does the heavy lifting. You make the call.

The system gives you a strong starting point while keeping the important creative decisions open to you, at every stage.

03 · Speed

The goal is simple: get to the finished mix sooner.

FREQ is built to reduce the hours spent chasing balance, space, and clarity, so more of your time can go back into making music.

Because time is part of the music too.

When someone uploads a song they've spent months creating and gets to a finished mix in minutes, something bigger happens. An independent artist can put more into recording. A producer can finish more ideas. A mixer can take on more work without giving every project the same long night. And maybe, just maybe, someone gets to shut the laptop a little earlier. That matters.

A note from Kushal

I didn't start FREQ because I wanted to build another piece of music software. I started it because I'd spent enough time mixing to know how much of the process was about listening, deciding, and second-guessing.

I kept wondering whether technology could take away some of the friction without taking away the person. FREQ is my answer to that question.

Kushal Jaju, Founder, FREQ
