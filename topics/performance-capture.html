Performance Capture

Micro-timing, captured dynamics, vibrato, articulation, and comping are versions of the same question: which parts of a real performance carry its identity, and how do recording and editing decisions preserve or erase them?

  • How Does Micro-Timing Change the Feel of a Performance?

    Two performances can contain exactly the same notes and still make you move differently. This article opens FREQ's Performance Capture cluster by treating micro-timing as a network of relationships between events, not a distance from a DAW grid.

  • Why Capture Dynamics at the Source Instead of Fixing Them Later?

    A ghost note is not simply a loud snare turned down. This article continues FREQ's Performance Capture cluster by treating dynamic expression as physical information a fader cannot fully recreate, and dynamic contrast as an arrangement decision made before the mix.

  • How Should You Capture Vibrato and Articulation?

    A microphone doesn't simply capture what note was played, it captures how that note began, developed and ended. This article continues FREQ's Performance Capture cluster by treating vibrato and articulation as expressive information that recording can preserve or accidentally erase.

  • Is Comping Really an Arrangement Decision?

    When you comp a performance, you aren't only choosing the best take, you're deciding which version of the performance the listener hears. This article closes FREQ's Performance Capture cluster by treating comping as orchestration of a performer's different expressive possibilities.
