Arrange for the Ear

Vocal Production

Applies the series' register and fusion/independence frameworks specifically to the voice — how register changes the vocal instrument itself, and how doubling, harmony placement, and depth build vocal hierarchy.

  • How Does Vocal Register Change Vocal Identity?

    A vocalist can sing the same melody in a different register and suddenly sound like a different character. This article opens FREQ's Vocal Production cluster by treating register as an arrangement decision that changes the voice's identity, not just its pitch.

  • How Do Vocal Doubles Change What We Hear?

    A doubled vocal can make a chorus feel enormous, or make it blurry and unfocused. This article continues FREQ's Vocal Production cluster by treating doubling as a decision about whether two performances should fuse into one object or remain two voices.

  • Why Ride a Vocal Before Compressing It?

    A compressor doesn't know that a singer whispered one word on purpose. This article continues FREQ's Vocal Production cluster by treating a pre-compression ride as a way of preserving intentional dynamic hierarchy before a compressor reshapes it.

  • How Does Vocal Depth Change Hierarchy?

    A vocal can be perfectly audible and still feel unimportant. This article closes FREQ's Vocal Production cluster by treating depth, not reverb, as an arrangement tool that organizes lead, harmony, ad-lib and background vocals into foreground, middle ground and background.
