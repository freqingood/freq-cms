Identity, Modulation & Space

Once a part has its own time and register, what makes the listener recognize it as a distinct source? This cluster looks at cut-through, vibrato, articulation, and spatial position as identity cues — reframing “cutting through” as a question of which cue is missing, not just how loud something is.

  • Why Does an Instrument Need More Than Volume to Cut Through?

    Turning a part up raises everything about it equally, including the parts of it that were never competing for attention. This article opens FREQ's Identity, Modulation and Space cluster by explaining why cutting through is a question of identity cues, not just level.

  • What Does Vibrato Add to an Instrument's Identity?

    A sustained note isn't necessarily a static one. This article looks at what vibrato and other forms of modulation add to a sustained sound's identity, when shared movement supports fusion, and when it just makes two parts harder to tell apart.

  • How Does Articulation Change an Instrument's Identity?

    The same pitch, on the same instrument, can feel like a completely different event depending on how it begins and how long it stays. This article looks at articulation as an arrangement decision: what an attack announces, what a short note frees up, and when contrast helps or hurts.

  • How Does Spatial Position Give an Instrument Its Identity?

    An instrument doesn't exist only as frequencies moving through time. It also occupies a place in the auditory scene. This article closes FREQ's Identity, Modulation and Space cluster by treating spatial position as an arrangement decision, not just a panning choice.
