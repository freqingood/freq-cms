Arrange for the Ear

Translation (Upstream)

Closes the Production stage of FREQ's arc. Loudness perception, mono compatibility, and playback-system survival are revisited here from the arrangement stage, not only the mix — because some translation problems start upstream of the mix bus.

  • Why Does Loudness Change What We Notice?

    A vocal can sound balanced at one listening level and feel wrong at another, without the mix changing at all. This article opens FREQ's Translation cluster by treating listening level as part of vocal hierarchy: it changes what the ear is most sensitive to, not just how loud everything is.

  • What Happens to Stereo Width When You Listen in Mono?

    A production can sound enormous in stereo and strangely empty in mono. This article continues FREQ's Translation cluster by treating stereo width as an arrangement decision: what information lives in the difference between channels, and what happens when that difference disappears.

  • How Does a Mix Survive Different Playback Systems?

    Translation doesn't begin when the mix is finished. It begins with what information the arrangement depends on. This article extends FREQ's mixing-side translation article upstream into arrangement, asking what makes a production robust across phones, headphones, cars and clubs.

  • Why Does a Mix Change When You Change Listening Level?

    Turn a mix up or down and the balance seems to shift, even though nothing was changed. This article extends FREQ's equal-loudness and 80 dB monitoring articles into arrangement, treating listening level as a diagnostic for hierarchy rather than a target to chase.

  • What Is Mastering Actually Checking?

    Mastering isn't a mysterious final repair stage. This closing article in FREQ's Arrange for the Ear series treats mastering as evaluation and preparation of a finished production, mapping exactly what it can fix, what it can only reduce, and what belongs upstream in arrangement, production or mixing.
