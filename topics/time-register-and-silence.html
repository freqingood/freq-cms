Time, Register & Silence

Two tools for letting simultaneous parts coexist without asking the listener to sort them out: separating them in time (staggered entrances, silence) or separating them in register (voicing, spacing). This cluster extends FREQ's frequency-masking and critical-bands articles upstream, from the mix into the arrangement.

  • How Does Silence Make an Arrangement Bigger?

    A rest is not a gap in the arrangement, it's a decision. This article opens FREQ's Time, Register and Silence cluster by explaining how removing a part can free perceptual budget, aid recovery from masking, and make the next entrance land harder than it would have otherwise.

  • Why Do Staggered Entrances Make an Arrangement Clearer?

    Two instruments can share register, timbre and importance and still feel crowded together or easy to follow apart. Timing is often the difference. This article explains how staggering entrances distributes information through time instead of removing it, and when synchronization is the better call.

  • How Does Register Solve Arrangement Problems Before EQ?

    Two parts can play at the same time without competing, if they occupy different parts of the pitch range. This article closes FREQ's Time, Register and Silence cluster by explaining how register spacing prevents masking upstream, before EQ has to carve space for it.
