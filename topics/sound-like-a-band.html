Sound Like a Band

How specific, well-known records and production eras got their sound, not as a claim that any mix can be reproduced exactly, but as a set of decisions and constraints you can borrow and apply to your own multitrack.

  • Chasing The Beatles' Sound: Recreating the 1966-67 Mixing Constraints

    How to chase The Beatles' 1966-67 sound without claiming to recreate it exactly. A mixing framework built on mono-derived sources, decisive panning, simple dynamics and removing modern stereo tools.

  • Chasing Pink Floyd's Sound: Recreating the 1972-75 Sonic Constraints

    How to chase Pink Floyd's 1972-75 sound without claiming to recreate it exactly. A mixing framework built on deliberate depth, strategic stereo, tape delay, plate reverb and restrained, non-modern dynamics.

  • Chasing Michael Jackson's Sound: Recreating the Thriller-Era Sonic Constraints

    How to chase Michael Jackson's Off the Wall / Thriller-era sound without claiming to recreate it exactly. A mixing framework built on transient preservation, fader rides over compression, a centred low end and forward vocal hierarchy.

  • Chasing Red Hot Chili Peppers' Sound: The Blood Sugar Sex Magik Constraints

    How to chase Red Hot Chili Peppers' early-90s sound without claiming to recreate it exactly. A mixing framework built on mono-centric placement, a forward bass, dry ambience and performance over processing.

  • Chasing Radiohead's Sound: The OK Computer Sonic Constraints

    How to chase Radiohead's OK Computer-era sound without claiming to recreate it exactly. A mixing framework built on an analog-first signal path, early digital experimentation and deliberate depth contrast.

  • Chasing Nirvana's Sound: The Nevermind Sonic Constraints

    How to chase Nirvana's Nevermind-era sound without claiming to recreate it exactly. A mixing framework built on real double-tracked guitars, a stable centre, powerful drums and preserved quiet-to-loud dynamics.

  • Chasing Led Zeppelin's Sound: The Led Zeppelin IV Sonic Constraints

    How to chase Led Zeppelin's IV-era sound without claiming to recreate it exactly. A mixing framework built on room-as-instrument recording, a mono-first mindset and restraint instead of processing.
