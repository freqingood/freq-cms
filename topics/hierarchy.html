Arrange for the Ear

Hierarchy

Not every instrument can be the lead at every moment. This cluster covers how arrangement establishes what matters most in a given bar or phrase, through contrast, repetition, processing load, and prioritization when several parts are changing at once.

  • Why Can't Every Instrument Be the Lead?

    Being audible and being important aren't the same thing. This article opens FREQ's Hierarchy cluster by explaining why an arrangement full of clearly recognizable parts can still leave the listener with nothing to follow.

  • How Does Arrangement Make One Part Feel More Important?

    A lead doesn't become a lead because of one magic property. Its importance emerges from the relationship between what it does and what everything around it does. This article continues FREQ's Hierarchy cluster by unpacking level, activity, repetition, register, density, contrast and timing as hierarchy cues.

  • Why Does Contrast Make an Arrangement Easier to Hear?

    An arrangement can contain individually recognizable instruments and still feel flat if too many of them behave similarly at once. This article continues FREQ's Hierarchy cluster by treating contrast as a relationship between sounds and moments, not a property of any single sound.

  • How Does Listener Processing Load Change an Arrangement?

    An arrangement can contain many good ideas, with every part clearly audible, and still feel difficult to follow. This article continues FREQ's Hierarchy cluster by treating processing load as a distinct concern from masking and density: not how much is playing, but how much the listener has to actively track at once.

  • How Does Repetition Give the Listener a Stable Reference?

    A repeated rhythm, harmonic pattern, register, timbre or phrase can establish a reference against which new information becomes easier to notice. This article continues FREQ's Hierarchy cluster by treating repetition as an arrangement tool for making change perceptible.

  • How Does an Arrangement Prioritize When Several Parts Are Changing at Once?

    Sometimes everything changes at once: the bass moves, the drums fill, the vocal enters, the guitar shifts voicings. This article closes FREQ's Hierarchy cluster by looking at how arrangement decides whether simultaneous change reads as several separate events or one larger transformation.
