## A clean session is half the mix

Most beginners lose their mix before they touch a fader. They open a session full of multitrack files named "Audio 1," "Audio 4," "Audio 9," with no order and no groups, and they start pushing levels at random.

**An organised session is the single biggest time-saver in mixing.** Before any balancing happens, your tracks should be named clearly, sorted by type, and grouped so related elements move together. FREQ automates most of this, so you start from order instead of chaos.

Here is how to set up a session properly.

---

## Step 1: Upload Your Multitrack Files

Open FREQ and upload your multitrack files into the Library. These are the separate recorded elements of your song: each vocal, each drum, the bass, the instruments.

Inside the Library you can:

- **Play the full session** to hear everything together
- **Solo any individual track** to hear it on its own

**Tip:** Export every track from the same start point in your recording tool, even if a track is silent at the beginning. Matching start points keep everything in time automatically when loaded.

---

## Step 2: Let FREQ Detect and Categorise

Manually sorting twenty tracks by type is slow and error-prone. FREQ does it for you.

**Instrument detection automatically identifies and categorises each track**; drums, bass, vocals, instruments. Your session organises itself by element type before you make a single decision.

Why this matters: knowing what each track is, at a glance, is the difference between a session you can mix and a puzzle you have to solve first.

---

## Step 3: Rename for Clarity

Clean track names make a session readable. "Lead Vox," "Snare Top," "Bass DI" tells you everything instantly. "Audio 7" tells you nothing.

Rename your tracks so the session reads like a map of your song. This pays off in every later step, because every mixing decision starts with knowing exactly what you are adjusting.

---

## Step 4: Group Related Tracks

Grouping lets you control related tracks as one unit. All your drum tracks, for example, can sit in a single drum group; raise the group level and the whole kit moves together.

**FREQ's suggested grouping proposes logical groups automatically**, so you do not have to build them from scratch. Accept the suggestions or adjust them to fit your song.

The important detail: **grouping retains each track's individual level and pan inside the group.** You get the simplicity of moving related elements together without losing the detailed balance underneath. The group is a control layer, not a merge; the individual tracks keep their settings.

---

## Why Grouping Changes How You Mix

Mixing one track at a time across a large session is slow and makes it hard to hear relationships. Grouping changes the unit of work.

- **Faster decisions:** Adjust a whole section's level in one move
- **Clearer relationships:** Balance the drum group against the vocal group, rather than chasing twenty individual faders
- **Cleaner depth assignment:** Groups can be placed in the mix as units, which makes the next stage; assigning depth; far simpler

Once your session is grouped and labelled, you are ready to assign depth and build a balance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I organise tracks before mixing?**  
Upload your multitrack files, name each track clearly, sort them by type, and group related elements so they move together. In FREQ, instrument detection categorises tracks automatically and suggested grouping proposes logical groups, so the session organises itself before you start mixing.

**What is track grouping in mixing?**  
Track grouping combines related tracks, such as all drums, into a single control unit so you can adjust them together. In FREQ, grouping retains each track's individual level and pan, so you control the group as a whole without losing the balance between the tracks inside it.

**What is instrument detection?**  
Instrument detection automatically identifies what each uploaded track is; drums, bass, vocals, or instruments; and categorises the session accordingly. This removes the manual work of sorting a session by element type before mixing.

**Why does session organisation matter for a mix?**  
A disorganised session slows every decision and hides the relationships between elements. Naming, sorting, and grouping tracks first means every later step, from balance to depth assignment, starts from a clear foundation rather than confusion.

**Do I need to group tracks to mix in FREQ?**  
Grouping is not required, but it makes mixing faster and clearer, especially on larger sessions. FREQ suggests groups automatically, so the setup cost is minimal and the workflow benefit is significant.
