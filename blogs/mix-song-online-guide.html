How to Mix a Song: The Complete Beginner-to-Finish Guide (Even on Headphones)

The hardest part of mixing is knowing where to start

Most mixing guides assume you already have a treated room, studio monitors, and a trained ear. Most people learning to mix have none of those. They have a song, a pair of headphones, and a session full of flat, unbalanced tracks that all sound like they are fighting each other.

This guide solves the starting problem first.

The fastest way to start a mix is to begin from a balance that already works. In FREQ , the Optimize Balance feature analyses your tracks and sets a calibrated starting balance in one action. Instead of staring at a wall of faders wondering which to move, you start from a mix that already sits together; then you shape it toward your vision. This is what makes mixing your own song realistic even on headphones.

Here is the full process, start to finish.

What You Need Before You Start

  • Your song split into separate tracks (multitrack files): vocals, drums, bass, instruments
  • A pair of headphones (monitors are a bonus, not a requirement)
  • A browser

That is it. FREQ runs entirely in a browser; no download, no installation, no plugins to buy.

Step 1: Upload Your Tracks

Open FREQ and upload your multitrack files into the Library. You can play the full session or solo any individual track to hear it on its own.

The Library is your raw material. Every track you uploaded sits here, ready to be organised, balanced, and shaped.

Tip: Upload clean, full-length multitrack files exported at the same start point. Consistent start points keep everything in time once they are loaded.

Step 2: Let FREQ Organise the Session

This is where mixing usually gets intimidating. FREQ removes most of that friction automatically.

Instrument detection: FREQ identifies and categorises your tracks; drums, bass, vocals, instruments; so your session is organised before you touch anything.

Renaming: Clean up track names so the session reads clearly. A well-labelled session is faster to mix.

Suggested grouping: FREQ proposes logical groups (for example, all drum tracks together). Grouping lets you control related tracks as one, while the platform retains each track's individual level and pan inside the group. You get the simplicity of a group without losing the detail underneath.

Why this matters: An organised session is half the mix. Most beginners lose hours to a messy session before any actual mixing happens. FREQ hands you a clean, grouped, labelled starting point.

Step 3: Assign Depths

This is the part that makes FREQ different from a standard mixer.

Depth-based mixing places every element along a front-to-back axis , not just left-to-right. In the Separation tab, you assign each track or group a depth position. Your most present elements; lead vocal, kick, snare; sit in the foreground. Harmonic and supporting elements sit in the midground. Room tone and texture sit in the background.

The result is a mix with perceived dimension. Elements feel placed at different distances, which creates separation and clarity without aggressive processing. Advanced routing is available here when you want finer control over signal flow.

In plain terms: Instead of everything sitting on one flat plane and competing, your mix gets depth. Things sit in front of or behind each other, the way they would in a real room.

Step 4: Optimize Balance (Start From a Mix That Works)

This is the step that makes self-mixing realistic.

Optimize Balance analyses your session and sets a calibrated starting balance automatically. Levels are set into a sensible relationship with each other, so the mix already sits together before you make a single manual move.

Why this changes everything for people mixing their own music:

  • You do not need a trained ear to start. The hardest skill in mixing is hearing what is wrong. Optimize Balance gives you a reference balance that is already close, so your job becomes shaping rather than rescuing.
  • You can mix on headphones. Because the starting balance is calibrated rather than guessed by eye, you are not relying on a perfect listening environment to find a workable foundation. You start from something trustworthy and adjust to taste.
  • It is a starting point, not a final answer. Optimize Balance gets you to a strong baseline fast. Everything after this is you making the mix yours.

This is the single most important feature for anyone learning to mix their own song. It removes the blank-session paralysis that stops most people before they begin.

Step 5: Refine the Balance

Now you shape. The Mixer gives you the core controls for every track and group:

  • Level: How loud each element sits
  • Pan: Left-to-right placement
  • Polarity: Flip phase to fix tracks that cancel each other out
  • Width: How wide an element spreads across the stereo field
  • Mute and Solo: Isolate or remove elements, individually or globally, to hear what each one contributes
  • Depth selection: Adjust the front-to-back placement you set earlier

Work in passes. Get levels sitting right first, then pan for space, then width for dimension. Solo sparingly; mixing is about how elements sound together, not in isolation.

Step 6: Add Character With the Console

FREQ 's Console section gives your mix tonal character through algorithm-driven processing. Every console character is built from algorithms, not borrowed from hardware; it is fully digital processing designed to add weight, warmth, and presence.

The era slider lets you move the tonal character of your mix across different sonic periods, applied across the depth lanes you have already set up. Different console types give you different starting characters to work from.

Use this to move a clean, correct mix toward one that has a recognisable feel. A balanced mix sounds right; a mix with character sounds intentional.

Step 7: Shape Dynamics and Space

The Advanced section is where the mix gains polish and control.

  • Compression: Ranges from hard to soft, controlling how tightly each element's dynamics are held
  • Reverb: Small, medium, and large options for placing elements in rooms of different sizes
  • FX: Drop in distortion, delay, and vibrato where a track needs movement or edge
  • Parallel compression: Adds density and power by blending a compressed signal under the original, without crushing the dynamics you want to keep
  • Global space settings: Set the overall sense of room the whole mix shares

A note on restraint: Add processing because the mix asks for it, not because the control is there. The depth and balance you built in the earlier steps are doing most of the work already.

Step 8: Master Your Song

Mastering is the final shaping pass that makes your song sound finished and consistent. FREQ 's Mastering section runs the full chain in one place:

  • EQ: Final tonal balance across the whole mix
  • Dynamics: Overall control of loud and soft
  • Glue: Binds the elements into one cohesive whole
  • Density: Adds fullness and body
  • 3D: Enhances the front-to-back dimension of the finished master
  • Tape: Adds smoothness and harmonic character
  • Stereo width: Sets how wide the final master sits
  • Final gain: Brings the master to its target loudness

The Quick Settings triangle lets you move between broad mastering characters fast, so you can audition different finished feels without resetting your chain.

What mastering does: It takes a good mix and makes it translate; sounding consistent and full on phones, laptops, earbuds, car speakers, and club systems alike.

Step 9: Check Your Loudness

Before you export, check that your song meets streaming targets.

FREQ shows a LUFS readout so you can see your integrated loudness; the measurement streaming platforms use to set playback volume. The Mono check lets you confirm your mix holds up when summed to mono, which matters for club systems, phone speakers, and many public playback systems.

Loudness in short: Most streaming platforms normalise to around -14 LUFS. Mixing toward that target means your song plays back at a consistent volume next to other tracks, without being turned down for being too loud or sounding weak for being too quiet.

Step 10: Commit, Version, and Create Your Mix

FREQ treats your mix like a version-controlled document.

  • Markers and Notes: Leave reference points and reminders inside the session
  • Commit: Save a snapshot of the current mix state
  • Versioning: Return to any committed state instantly, so you can experiment without fear of losing a version you liked
  • Wizard (auto-save): Keeps your work saved as you go
  • Create Mix: Render your finished mix to a final output

Why versioning matters: It lets you mix boldly. Try the aggressive idea. If it does not work, roll back to the last commit in one action. You are never more than one click from a version you trusted.

Step 11: Export and Share

Finished mixes live in My Mixes. From here you can:

  • Play back and download your final track
  • See final track measurements to confirm loudness and specs
  • Rename mixes to keep your catalogue organised
  • Generate a streaming link to share your song directly, no file transfer needed

That link is how you send a finished song to a collaborator, a client, or a listener; they open it and hear it, instantly.

Can You Really Mix a Song on Headphones?

Yes; and this is worth saying plainly because so many people believe they cannot.

The traditional barrier to headphone mixing is that without a calibrated listening environment, you cannot trust your starting balance. FREQ removes that barrier at the source. Optimize Balance gives you a calibrated foundation that does not depend on your room. From there, depth-based placement creates separation you can clearly hear on headphones, where front-to-back positioning is often easier to perceive than on speakers.

You will not need perfect monitors to make a song that translates. You need a trustworthy starting point and tools that let you shape it. That is exactly what this workflow gives you.
