Your Mix, Your Rules ; Why the Artist-Engineer Handoff Is Broken (And How to Fix It)

The handoff loses something. Always.

You send your multitrack files. You write a brief. You send reference tracks.

The mix comes back technically clean; emotionally approximate.

Three revision rounds later, you are not fixing the mix. You are trying to rebuild the context that got lost in the handoff.

This is not a talent problem. It is a workflow problem. And there is a better one.

Two Paths. One Platform. Zero Information Lost.

The FREQ Workflow

Path 1 ; Mix It Yourself

Who it is for: Independent artists, bedroom producers, anyone with a vision and a tight budget.

How it works:

  1. Upload your multitrack files to FREQ
  2. Assign each element to a depth lane; kick and bass in the foreground, pads and room tone in the distance, everything else between
  3. Set width and positional placement using the panning controls
  4. Apply algorithm-driven console character to individual lanes
  5. Commit your mix state; version it like a document
  6. Export

No technical background required. The interface is built around depth and dimension decisions, not signal flow diagrams.

Path 2 ; Set the Foundation, Let the Engineer Finish It

Who it is for: Artists who want professional polish without losing creative control. Engineers who want a better starting point than a PDF brief and a folder of multitrack files.

How it works:

  1. Artist uploads multitrack files to FREQ and assigns depth positions; foreground, mid, background
  2. Artist builds a rough balance that reflects their creative intent
  3. Artist shares the session link with their mixing engineer
  4. Engineer opens the same session in a browser; no file transfer, no format issues
  5. Engineer re-assigns depths, refines the depth field, applies console character and tonal shaping using FREQ 's algorithm-driven processing
  6. Engineer renders the final output directly from the platform

What the engineer receives: Not a brief. Not a reference track. An actual creative document; depth assignments, rough balance, positional intent, all editable and visible. The engineer knows exactly what the artist was going for before a single fader is touched.

What the artist receives: A final mix that starts from their intent, not from a blank session. The engineer's expertise applied to the artist's foundation; not to a cold interpretation of an email.

Why This Changes the Artist-Engineer Relationship

The traditional mixing workflow asks the engineer to reconstruct the artist's vision from incomplete information. Reference tracks. Adjectives. Revision notes that approximate things that resist description.

FREQ changes the starting point entirely. The engineer receives creative data; depth positions, rough balance decisions, lane assignments. This communicates intent with a specificity no brief can match.

For the artist: Your creative decisions are preserved and visible. Revisions become adjustments, not reconstructions.

For the mixing engineer: You receive a session with actual creative context. Less time decoding the brief. More time doing the technical work. Your expertise is applied to a foundation, not spent rebuilding one from guesswork.

For the collaboration: Both parties work in the same environment, on the same session, with the same depth vocabulary. The gap closes because the medium is shared.

What Depth-Based Mixing Actually Does

Most digital mixing organises sound on a left-right stereo plane. FREQ adds a front-to-back axis.

Every element in your mix is assigned a depth position. The foreground holds your most present elements; lead vocals, kick, snare. The midground carries harmonic elements. The background holds room tone and texture.

The result is a mix with perceived dimension; not just frequency separation. Elements feel placed, not just balanced.

This depth foundation is what the artist builds and the engineer refines. It is a shared creative language both parties can see, adjust, and agree on.

Algorithm-Driven Console Processing

FREQ 's console section assigns algorithm-driven character to individual depth lanes.

This is not a recreation of any specific piece of hardware. It is digital processing built to give each depth lane a distinct tonal character; the kind of weight and presence that separates a mix that sounds clean from one that sounds considered.

The artist sets rough lane character during the foundation pass. The engineer refines or reassigns character in the same session. The decision is layered, not overwritten.

Mix Versioning ; Mix Without Fear

Every meaningful state of a FREQ session can be committed and restored; like version control for audio.

For the artist: Experiment freely during the foundation pass. If something breaks the session, roll it back in one click.

For the engineer: Commit states at key points in the mix. Present the artist with a before-and-after. Restore the previous state if they prefer it.

No more "final_mix_v7_ACTUALLY_FINAL" in a shared folder. The session history is built into the platform.

Real-Time Collaboration

FREQ supports live session sharing. Artist and engineer can be in the same session simultaneously, from different locations.

The host broadcasts mixer state, playback, and a shared cursor to remote participants. A QR code invite makes the session shareable in seconds.

Use case: The artist watches the engineer work in real time. Feedback is immediate and specific; "move the guitar further back" rather than approximated across an email chain three days later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need audio engineering training to use FREQ ?
No. FREQ is designed for artists who understand their music deeply but are not trained audio engineers. The depth-based interface translates positional and creative decisions into mixing actions. You place sounds where they feel right; the platform handles the technical layer.

Can a professional mixing engineer use FREQ for client work?
Yes. FREQ is built to support the artist-engineer workflow. Engineers receive a session with the artist's creative intent already mapped; depth positions, rough balance, positional decisions. This replaces the brief as a starting point and significantly reduces revision cycles.

What is depth-based mixing?
Depth-based mixing organises sound along a front-to-back axis in addition to the standard left-right stereo plane. Elements are assigned depth positions; foreground, midground, background. This creates perceived dimension in a mix without relying purely on frequency separation or reverb. It is FREQ 's foundational mixing philosophy.

Is browser-based mixing professional quality?
FREQ combines a browser-based control interface with cloud DSP. The mixing controls run in the browser; the signal processing runs at the infrastructure level. Quality is not limited by browser capabilities.

How does session sharing work in FREQ ?
The artist shares a session link or QR code with their mixing engineer. The engineer opens the session in a browser; no file transfer required. Both parties see the same session state; depth assignments, fader positions, console character assignments. The engineer works directly in that session to produce the final output.

How is FREQ different from other online mixing platforms?
Most online mixing platforms are file-transfer services; you upload your tracks, an engineer downloads them, and a new file comes back. FREQ is a shared creative environment. Artist and engineer work in the same session, with full visibility into each other's decisions. The depth-based interface and algorithm-driven processing have no direct equivalent in browser-based tools.
